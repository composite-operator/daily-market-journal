Regime Map

The market has two live paths. The conventional path is a choppy five-down, two-up sequence that keeps frustrating shorts before the next leg lower. The direct path is a nested one-two series that can turn into a three-of-three liquidation wave without giving a polite rally first. That direct path is not the base case for every chart, but it is now live enough to respect.

The risk tape is losing support outside one narrow area: semiconductors and memory. If Micron, SMH, AMD, and related memory names squeeze one more time, tech can get another pump and delay the downside. If that sleeve fails, the market loses its main support beam while software, consumer discretionary, crypto proxies, high beta, and some mega-cap platforms are already leaning lower.

Regime Rules Learned

  • A box is not a buy when divergence conditions are missing. Large boxes need time-frame confirmation, especially on indexes, metals, miners, and potash/fertilizer setups.
  • When an obvious commodity level is all over the market's radar, assume the first bounce can be a trap unless related confirmation lines up.
  • If dollar strength, yen weakness, and volatility expansion align, the tape can shift from ordinary pullback to liquidation very quickly.
  • One narrow leadership sleeve can delay downside but cannot validate a broad risk-on regime by itself.
  • Energy correlation matters. Owning several energy names does not diversify the portfolio if the same oil/gas factor drives them all.
  • Do not chase crypto miners after uncorrected runs. A completed fifth wave can give back far more than late buyers expect.
  • Middle-zone trading is where both bulls and bears get chopped. Let price either reach the box or reclaim the fault line.
