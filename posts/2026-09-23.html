The working posture is defensive until the rate shock stabilizes, with selective support tests still alive. A bounce in rate-sensitive shares remains possible. It is not evidence that the Fed must rescue the market, nor that a support zone forces yields to peak. Conversely, a sharp down day does not establish a terminal market collapse. The escalation test is sustained yield pressure accompanied by failed equity support, broader credit stress, and expanding volatility.

Research Rules

  1. Separate growth strength from easier conditions. Test nominal activity, inflation, and real yields independently; positive growth data can tighten the required return on equities.
  2. A support cluster is a hypothesis, not a causal cap on yields. Log whether TLT, regional banks, and dividend-sensitive shares actually turn together before upgrading the relief regime.
  3. Score conditional forecasts by their triggers. Record the level, horizon, failure condition, and final stated interpretation before observing the outcome. Do not count both mutually exclusive branches as a win.
  4. Make reclaim research finite. Predefine the trigger, reversal-low stop, maximum attempts, slippage, and total loss budget. Compare the result with a one-entry baseline out of sample.
  5. Preserve records when hypotheses change. A new second-chance zone does not rewrite a historical fill, remove a loss, or widen a live stop.
  6. Measure correlated exposure. Several crypto tokens or AI-linked names can represent the same liquidity bet. Aggregate risk before judging each setup's apparent reward.

Bottom Line

The September 22 broadening test has not passed. Rising yields, the dollar, and oil have raised the hurdle for it. Selected rebounds remain possible, especially where structures are close to support, but there is no confirmed policy rescue or completed market bottom. Keep tactical reaction trades separate from a durable easing thesis, preserve actual stops, and require cross-market evidence before declaring either renewed expansion or systemic breakdown.
