Regime Map

The macro argument is that the economy is now being carried more by business-to-business AI capital spend than by the consumer. GDP looked better partly for mechanical reasons while consumer spending was revised lower. AI capex is keeping the economy out of recession for now, but that also means any deceleration in AI spending threatens the liquidity engine supporting the equity bubble.

Inflation is still too high for a clean easing story. Core PCE was hot enough to keep rate-hike expectations alive. Fed commentary, ISM manufacturing, unemployment, and weekly jobless claims become the short-week catalysts. A cooling labor print can produce relief because traders will price less policy pressure; a strong labor print reinforces higher-for-longer or renewed hike risk.

The most important asset is oil. The current oil decline helps defensive and rate-sensitive groups temporarily, but the read expects oil and rates to rise again unless the economy is already sliding into hidden demand destruction. If oil bottoms, energy and services can work, but the broad market loses the lower-inflation relief valve.

Regime Rules Learned

  • When business investment replaces the consumer as the main growth driver, the market becomes more exposed to capex-cycle fatigue.
  • Lower oil and lower rates can temporarily help defensives, healthcare, utilities, and real estate, but those gains reverse if oil/rates rise again.
  • AI infrastructure can still rally from support while the larger narrative degrades; distinguish reaction trades from durable leadership.
  • Boxes in parabolic sectors are high risk on first touch; a later double-correction box is often cleaner than the first support hit.
  • A lower dollar can trigger the final speculative peak, but a dollar failure later can also become inflationary rather than purely bullish.
  • In a coming rotationary/secular sideways market, do not fall in love with household-name tech. Rotate into sectors where wave-three behavior is emerging.
  • Short-week holiday liquidity can exaggerate whipsaws; middle-zone trades should be smaller, faster, or avoided.
