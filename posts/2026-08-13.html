Research Rules Learned

  • Separate a below-expectation inflation release from low inflation. The surprise can support a trade without changing the regime.
  • Do not top-tick a mature trend. Divergence defines vulnerability; a failed cycle or impulsive reversal supplies timing.
  • Treat lower long-end rates as temporary support until the larger rate structure fails. Relief and regime reversal are different claims.
  • Require channel agreement before declaring liquidation. Dollar strength, rising rates, a volatility breakout, and weakening breadth must reinforce one another.
  • Separate structural themes from tactical entries. Steel, mining, energy, and defense can have durable demand while current prices remain poor entries.
  • Use ratios as confirmation, not prophecy. Gold-silver, crude-silver, and uranium-silver relationships help test rotation after price reaches a decision area.
  • Preserve the imported board when speech and tracking list disagree. Put invalidations, event risk, date conflicts, and apparent level-copy errors in Needs Review.

Bottom Line

The market has not entered confirmed liquidation. Softer long-end rates can still finance one more late-cycle extension through small caps, the Nasdaq, and selected energy or semiconductor structures. The relief remains conditional because producer inflation is persistent, the dollar is firm, the Dow is weak, and volatility is compressed. The bullish branch requires orderly pullbacks, improving breadth, and no joint breakout in the dollar, rates, and VIX. The defensive branch confirms when temporary rate relief ends and those tightening channels rise together. Until then, the correct posture is selective, level-driven, and careful not to mistake extra time for a new easy-liquidity regime.
