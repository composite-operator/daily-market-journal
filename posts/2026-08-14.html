Research Rules Learned

  • Do not top-tick a mature bull cycle. Divergence and extreme positioning define vulnerability; failed cycles and impulsive downside supply timing.
  • Use early-week semiconductor behavior to distinguish a terminal squeeze from a corrective rebound. A broad surge and a lazy advance imply different index paths.
  • Keep risk horizons separate. A tactical stop, a second-chance accumulation area, and a higher-timeframe thesis invalidation can all coexist without replacing one another.
  • Require small-cap breadth to validate the final risk-on extension. Megacap-only highs are weaker evidence than synchronized IWM and SMH participation.
  • Require channel agreement for a defensive turn. Dollar strength, higher long-end rates, volatility expansion, and weaker breadth should reinforce one another.
  • Let a true third wave prove itself through shallow pullbacks. Deep overlap in uranium or another breakout sleeve reduces urgency and changes the count.
  • Separate structural themes from immediate entries. Robotics, uranium, energy, and emerging markets can have durable flow support while current prices remain poor locations.
  • Treat 62,000 Bitcoin as a liquidity control, not an isolated crypto level. A sustained break would inform the broader speculative-risk regime.
  • Search for the next capital destination by asking what has not already gone parabolic, but require a defined correction and structure before entry. Japan, robotics, and freight are research sleeves, not permission to chase.
  • Use freight as an inflation-transmission monitor. A Baltic Dry breakout matters only when tanker breadth, commodity flows, and geopolitical bottlenecks confirm it.
  • Do not collapse related technology trades into one thesis. Memory, data-center capex, robotics, networking, and industrial automation can occupy different cycle positions.
