Research Rules

  1. Separate the policy fact from the market inference. The hike is documented; a forced recession, future easing, or sector rotation is a thesis to test.
  2. Require joint confirmation. Read dollar, long yields, volatility, financials, small caps, and technology breadth together after the first reaction.
  3. Do not equate a rebound with repair. A choppy advance can coexist with an unfinished decline; use subsequent lows and leadership participation to discriminate.
  4. Keep supply shock and demand shock distinct. Falling energy equities with resilient copper and natural gas is not the same evidence as broad commodity failure.
  5. Maintain board provenance. Filled rows, original entries, second-chance areas, and spoken chart levels have different meanings. Never merge them silently.
  6. Make dramatic paths falsifiable. A calendar crash analogy needs a failed rally, broad breakdown, and persistent volatility; without those it remains only a stress case.

Bottom Line

The rate increase tightened the policy backdrop, but the initial market response split rather than collapsed. Technology resilience and firm copper/natural gas complicate a simple recession reading; financials, discretionary, small caps, and the dollar argue against declaring relief. The near-term test is whether a rebound gains broad participation while yields and volatility ease. If it instead fails under a firm dollar and weak cyclicals, the defensive branch strengthens. No calendar-based crash call or unverified supply report is promoted to a conclusion.
