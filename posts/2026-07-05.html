Regime Map

The labor report changed the near-term policy tone without proving a clean soft landing. Payrolls were weak after revisions, unemployment only improved because the labor force shrank, and markets responded by easing near-term hike pressure. That helps dollar, rates, metals, crypto, and high beta tactically, but it is not enough by itself to erase the risk that growth is slowing underneath the headline.

AI is the pressure point. The new risk is not only whether the biggest platform names can rally; it is whether excess AI capacity turns capital expenditure into a cash-flow problem. If unused GPU capacity gets rented out by hyperscalers, pure compute-rental businesses and second-derivative AI infrastructure names can face pricing pressure. The useful test is free cash flow: if the AI buildout is real demand, cash generation should follow. If it is overbuild, dilution, debt, margin pressure, and down-round style repricing become the transmission path.

Oil is the weekly macro switch. Sub-$70 crude can look like inflation relief, but the longer it stays weak, the more it starts to imply demand stress. A reversal through 77-80 would flip the read back toward inflation pressure, higher rates, and risk-asset stress. That makes energy, metals, crypto, and big-cap technology part of the same capital-flow equation rather than separate charts.
