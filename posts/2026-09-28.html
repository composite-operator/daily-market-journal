Rates, Currency, and Commodity Tests

The 20-year yield structure permits an ending now, while daily and four-hour momentum leave room for another push. That is a timing disagreement, not a settled bond bottom. One possible sequence is a bond rebound, temporary relief in rate-sensitive equities, and then another yield high. Another is a more durable turn. Neither requires an assumed emergency policy rescue.

Keep price direction straight: rising yields generally mean falling existing fixed-rate bond prices. A forecast of another yield high is not a forecast of another TLT high. Treasury duration, housing affordability, equity discount rates, and refinancing costs are related but distinct transmission channels.

The currency thesis also needs precision. Falling USDJPY means a stronger yen. DXY measures six currencies: euro, yen, pound, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona, and Swiss franc. It is not an emerging-market currency basket, and one yen move cannot force every other component to move with it. ICE contract specification. The revised yen hypothesis is a possible approaching turn after another bounce, not an established intervention or a confirmed currency regime change.

Crude oil below 96.85 keeps the nearer corrective branch open; the imported lower CL crude-oil candidate remains 78.32 / 67.26. Gold near 4,000 is a possible tactical reaction reference before another low, not a guaranteed cycle bottom. Silver's shorter trade and older accumulation thesis must retain their separate risk limits. Copper, COPX, and XME remain useful tests of cyclical participation.

VIX and volatility-product patterns still need an actual breakout with failed equity recovery. A compressed pattern alone does not establish a crash date, and leveraged UVIX levels cannot be substituted for VIX levels.

  1. Test the rate channel over the next five sessions. Record whether lower Treasury yields coincide with TLT recovery and wider participation in IWM, XHB, and KRE. If only XLU rises, classify the result as defensive rotation, not broad repair.
  2. Separate cancellation from failure. An unfilled QQQ or CVS idea that is withdrawn is a cancelled opportunity. BA's stopped strategy is a different outcome. Neither is silently deleted from the historical sample.
  3. Require activation before scoring. Z above 29 and OPEN above 2.50 must actually reclaim those levels before measuring an activated thesis. A below-entry quote is not sufficient.
  4. Freeze each forecast version. Preserve the invalidated prior semiconductor branch and NOW's revised count. A new interpretation starts a new test; it does not repair the earlier prediction.
  5. Test retail breadth, not a chosen winner. Compare XRT and the full preselected retail set over five and ten sessions. Include failed candidates and compare with SPY before declaring early consumer leadership.
  6. Keep model discovery separate from performance. Agreement between an automated screen and manual inspection is not a return study. Archive candidates before subsequent prices are known and evaluate with fixed rules and realistic execution costs.

Bottom Line

Financing pressure keeps the broad market defensive, but the evidence is not uniformly bearish. The next useful improvement is a bond-led recovery that spreads into rate-sensitive breadth; the failure case is another yield high accompanied by failed rebounds. Retire the explicit cancellations, preserve the stopped outcomes, and treat selective retail and security strength as testable exceptions rather than an all-clear signal.
