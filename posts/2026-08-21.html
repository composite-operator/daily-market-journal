Macro Flow Tape

Dollar and policy credibility: The Treasury buyback mattered more as information than as liquidity. It signaled discomfort with the long end and revived expectations that larger interventions may eventually follow. The dollar now sits at a fork: a continued decline would loosen global conditions and support commodities, emerging markets, metals, and crypto; a rebound would restore the prior path of higher rates first and policy relief later.

Commodity and freight inflation: Broad commodity baskets are testing breakout structures while Panama Canal restrictions and reported material bottlenecks raise shipping costs. These channels can transmit inflation beyond crude oil. The thesis strengthens if freight indexes, agriculture, industrial inputs, and energy advance together; it weakens if the move remains concentrated in geopolitical oil.

AI capital expenditure: Data-center construction faces growing political resistance over electricity, water, noise, and limited local employment. Additional permitting, utility, and infrastructure costs would pressure hyperscaler free cash flow and reduce the expected return on memory, power, and component spending. This is a medium-term capex risk, not proof that current projects stop immediately.

Oil and metals: Oil remains constructive toward roughly 90.48 and then potentially 106, but a completed fifth wave could first retrace toward the low-80s. Gold and silver can make another high while the dollar weakens. Their larger pullback thesis survives unless the dollar breaks structurally and the gold-silver ratio loses its remaining confirmation levels.

Event calendar: Nvidia earnings on Wednesday are the principal equity catalyst. GDP also arrives Wednesday, jobless claims Thursday, and PCE Friday. The sequence can reprice growth, inflation, and duration within three sessions, so pre-event chart levels should be treated as scenarios rather than guarantees.

Geopolitical risk: Claims of imminent attacks or covert platforms remain unverified and must not be treated as a base case. The usable market observation is narrower: Middle East escalation risk maintains an oil-volatility premium, while any confirmed disruption to shipping or energy infrastructure would tighten that channel rapidly.
