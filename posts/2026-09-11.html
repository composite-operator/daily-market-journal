Research Rules

  1. Separate the first reaction from the regime verdict. Friday's rally is evidence of demand, not proof that the correction or liquidity problem is over.
  2. Classify inflation by Current read. Energy-led headline inflation and broad core inflation have different sector effects, but both can constrain policy.
  3. Read policy through transmission. The decision matters only through the dollar, the yield curve, volatility, credit, breadth, and leadership.
  4. Do not chase a mature impulse. Strong energy and commodity trends can remain bullish while offering poor new entry geometry.
  5. Keep structure and timing separate. A box defines an area. Divergence, momentum, breadth, and the invalidation determine whether the setup is ready.
  6. Reconcile the record before acting. Duplicate rows, conflicting statuses, future fill dates, and split-distorted levels are data defects, not signals.
  7. Write both branches. Relief requires lower oil, lower yields, compressed volatility, and broader participation. Failure is the joint continuation of oil, yields, and volatility with weak breadth.

Bottom Line

Friday's rally kept the immediate tape alive, but it did not settle the larger question. Core inflation is easing while energy inflation is accelerating, long rates remain restrictive, and small caps still lag. That mix supports whipsaw and sector dispersion rather than a clean all-clear. The constructive branch requires oil to consolidate, long yields to turn, volatility to remain contained, and breadth to expand after the Fed. If oil and long rates resume higher while volatility breaks out, the relief rally becomes a trap and portfolio defense takes priority.
