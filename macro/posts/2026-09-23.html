Strong Growth Raises the Price of Risk

Dated 2026-09-23

Macro-flow field notes for traders: regime, liquidity, cross-asset pressure, sector transmission, and portfolio risk mechanics.

Market Lens

Composite Operator: Macro Lensing

2026-09-23: The economy can strengthen while the market's tolerance for expensive assets weakens. September's flash PMI rose to 58.4 from 56.0, with stronger activity and renewed cost pressure. The inference is not recession. It is that growth has become less helpful to equity valuations when it also pushes the expected cost of money higher.

Reuters' session recap reported the 10-year Treasury yield near 5.10%, lower major equity indexes, and simultaneous gains in the dollar and oil. Energy alone finished positive among S&P sectors. The previous session's question was whether leadership could broaden. The new question is whether that broadening can survive a renewed discount-rate shock.

Regime assessment: tighter financial conditions within resilient nominal growth. This differs from a proven credit accident or generalized funding shortage. The valuation channel is already visible; the balance-sheet and refinancing channels must be tested separately. Chart support can identify where the thesis faces a decision. It cannot make the macro constraint disappear.

Research Rules

  1. Record the cause of a yield move. Distinguish growth repricing, inflation repricing, term-premium changes, and funding stress where data permit. Label the attribution uncertain when they cannot be separated.
  2. Use an evidence stack, not a visual analogy. Pair rate-sensitive equity support with actual yields, credit spreads, and breadth. A chart zone is a test location, not proof of the macro mechanism.
  3. Test whether better growth raises or lowers risk appetite. Condition the result on real yields, costs, and earnings revisions; do not assign a fixed bullish sign to a strong activity release.
  4. Separate rate relief from credit relief. Compare bond rallies with credit and earnings changes before classifying them as constructive easing.
  5. Freeze conditional forecasts before scoring them. Store the final thesis, trigger, horizon, alternative, and invalidation. A later relabeling is a revision, not evidence that the original forecast succeeded.
  6. Account for path and correlated losses. Test reclaim strategies with a fixed attempt cap, total risk budget, costs, and an untouched comparison period. Aggregate positions that share the same liquidity factor.

Bottom Line

The market is not facing a lack of growth; it is facing a higher price for financing that growth. That distinction argues for tighter risk discipline without assuming a terminal collapse. A durable improvement needs calmer yields, credible cost relief, and broader participation that survives the credit test. Until those conditions emerge, selective rebounds are tactical events rather than proof that the liquidity backdrop has turned friendly.
