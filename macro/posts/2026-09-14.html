Scenario Matrix

Orderly repair: Oil consolidates, long yields reverse, volatility compresses, and software strength broadens into semiconductors, banks, and small caps. This weakens the defensive thesis even if the first Fed headline is ambiguous.

Rotational whipsaw: Large software names hold while semiconductors and small caps lag. Index relief persists, but internal financial conditions do not improve enough to support a full regime upgrade.

Supply-led squeeze: Oil advances while long yields and inflation expectations climb. Producers may benefit, but consumers, transport, housing, leveraged borrowers, and duration-heavy growth face simultaneous pressure.

Liquidation test: Volatility breaks out with lower equity lows and a disorderly currency or credit move. That combination, not a weak session alone, is the confirmation for active portfolio defense.

Disinflationary failure: Oil and commodities fall because demand is weakening rather than supply normalizing. Lower headline inflation would not be a constructive sign unless breadth and credit recover.

Research Rules

  1. Measure the joint move. Classify the regime with oil, yields, the dollar, the yen, volatility, credit, and breadth rather than a single index close.
  2. Separate supply inflation from demand strength. Higher oil can raise headline inflation while weakening consumers and cyclicals.
  3. Separate rotation from repair. Software outperformance only upgrades the tape when hardware, small caps, and funding-sensitive sectors join it.
  4. Read policy through transmission. A decision or forecast is not confirmed until currencies, yields, volatility, credit, and leadership respond.
  5. Distinguish secular capacity from the current equity cycle. AI infrastructure needs can remain real while crowded near-term valuations reset.
  6. Keep relief and liquidation tests symmetric. Falling oil, yields, and volatility with expanding breadth falsify the defensive case; the opposite joint move strengthens it.
  7. Use horizons explicitly. A Fed-week rebound, a seasonal correction, and a multi-year capital cycle require different confirmation and failure tests.

Bottom Line

The market is in a fragile rotation test, not a proven crash and not a repaired risk regime. Software strength can support relief, but semiconductor and small-cap weakness leave the long-rate and oil channels in control of the larger decision. Durable repair needs lower yields, contained oil and volatility, and wider participation after the Fed. If those pressures rise together while breadth narrows, the defensive thesis gains evidence.
