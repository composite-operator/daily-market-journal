The selloff was triggered by leadership earnings weakness, but the deeper pressure comes from rising oil, rising rates, yen instability, volatility expansion, and a narrowing tolerance for distant-profit narratives.

Regime Map

The tape has shifted from “support can still work” to “support must prove itself quickly.” The market is not in a confirmed crash yet because semiconductors have not fully failed and some internal pockets still show resilience. But the stress stack is heavier now: cash-flow preference, energy inflation, long-end pressure, FX instability, and volatility breakout are all moving in the same defensive direction.

The next regime decision sits in the semiconductor and Korea/Asia hardware channel. If that channel holds, QQQ can still form a controlled low and produce one more rally. If it fails, the selloff stops being only mega-cap earnings damage and becomes broader liquidation risk.

Macro Flow Tape

Cash flow is becoming the market's filter. In a rising-rate, rising-oil environment, investors are less willing to fund stories that require patience. This is why leadership earnings misses matter: they expose which parts of the index were priced for execution perfection.

Semiconductors remain the release valve. The broad market can still stabilize if the semiconductor channel holds. But if hardware/AI leadership breaks while volatility and dollar-yen stress rise, the market can move from orderly repricing into forced selling.

Currency stress is the hidden accelerant. A yen-confidence problem can turn a normal equity support test into an overnight liquidity event. The support-support zone map remains useful, but FX can override the slower equity rhythm.

Research Rules

  • When oil and rates rise together, downgrade speculative-growth support unless cash-flow leadership confirms.
  • Do not call a broad crash until semiconductors, volatility, FX stress, and breadth align.
  • Treat SMH/EWY as a gating variable for QQQ. Semiconductor failure can invalidate otherwise clean index support zones.
  • Support reached by orderly decline is different from support reached by gap-down liquidation.
  • Currency stress can outrank equity wave structure. Yen-carry risk requires a separate override flag.
  • Volatility breakout is more actionable when paired with leadership earnings failure and higher long-end yields.

Bottom Line

The market is in a defensive confirmation test with crash risk rising but not yet fully proven. If semiconductors and EWY hold, QQQ can still form a controlled low and stage one more rally. If they fail while oil, rates, volatility, and yen stress keep rising, the regime shifts from earnings-led repricing into broader liquidation risk. Until that decision resolves, the posture is selective, cash-flow-aware, and hostile to unsupported growth narratives.
