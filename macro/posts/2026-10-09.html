The Long End Stayed Restrictive

The Treasury's official October 9 curve put the 10-year yield at 5.24%, the 20-year at 5.65%, and the 30-year at 5.60%. Compared with October 8, the 10-year rose two basis points, the 20-year rose one, and the 30-year was unchanged. U.S. Treasury daily curve.

Inference: the discount-rate burden did not ease enough to confirm a broad risk-on regime. Long-duration assets still need either lower yields or stronger earnings evidence.

CPI Week Is the Next Regime Test

BLS schedules September CPI and real earnings for Wednesday, October 14 at 8:30 a.m. ET, followed by September PPI on Thursday, October 15 at 8:30 a.m. ET. BLS October release calendar.

The sequence matters because the market is already carrying elevated long yields. A softer pair of releases can create room for a bond rebound and an equity extension. A firm pair can push the rate-and-oil branch toward a broader correction.

Oil Is the Inflation Multiplier

Oil is not yet confirming a new trend, but it is close to a structural decision. The working map uses roughly 94 as the near-term gate, 106.99 as the larger bullish gate, and 78.64 as the lower candidate area.

Inference: higher oil and higher long yields would reinforce each other. Sideways or lower oil would let the market absorb restrictive rates for longer.

Breadth Is the Missing Confirmation

SPY retained a constructive short-term structure, while QQQ and IWM remained corrective. SMH held near 600, but a break opens a deeper move toward roughly 571. IWM needs a move above about 284.70 to end its immediate downtrend.

This split is important. A durable extension should not depend only on a few large-cap names while small caps remain rate-constrained.
