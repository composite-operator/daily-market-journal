Regime Map

The dominant near-term mechanism is simultaneous pressure from oil and the long end. Higher energy prices preserve inflation risk, while higher long yields restrict small caps, housing, dividend proxies, speculative growth, and leveraged balance sheets. Equity support can still produce violent rebounds, but those rebounds remain low-quality until rates soften and former leadership stabilizes.

The medium-term branch is more constructive. REITs, real estate, infrastructure, and selected country ETFs are beginning to form higher-timeframe setups that would benefit from lower long rates or direct duration support. This is an anticipatory map, not proof that easing has started. The alternative branch is a disorderly bond repricing that overwhelms equity support and keeps the tape defensive.

Claims of coordinated currency intervention, a hidden equity-support mandate, and imminent military strikes were not treated as facts. They remain hypotheses or event-risk scenarios until independently confirmed.
